        How Talya works

        1. Record your calm voice once. Check my tone walks you through it. This is your baseline, the "normal you" that everything is compared to.
        2. Check a real moment. Say something the way you actually said it, or are about to say it. Talya shows how it may come across.
        3. Practice one skill. Drills give you a clear goal, like "say it slower," and tell you when you hit it.
        4. Prep hard talks. Build a calm opening line and rehearse it before the real conversation.

        What each measurement means

        Pace

        How fast you talk, counted in syllables per second. "Breakfast" is two syllables.

        Typical
        About 3.6 to 5.2 per second in everyday conversation.
        Faster
        Often heard as rushed, impatient, defensive or anxious. Your partner gets less room to answer.
        Slower
        Usually heard as calm and careful. Very slow can sound tired, sad or withholding.

        Pitch

        How high or low your voice sits. Talya measures it in hertz (Hz), the number of times your vocal cords vibrate per second. Many men speak around 85 to 155 Hz and many women around 165 to 255 Hz, but everyone differs.

        Because voices differ so much, Talya compares your pitch with your own calm voice, in semitones. A semitone is one step on a piano.

        0 to 1
        Same as calm. Hard to hear any difference.
        +2 or more
        Noticeably higher. Common with stress, excitement, upset or pleading.
        −2 or less
        Noticeably lower. Can sound serious, tired or flat.

        Pitch movement

        How much your voice goes up and down while you talk. This is what makes speech sound expressive instead of robotic.

        Under 1.5
        Near monotone. Often read as bored, tired, cold or holding back, even when you are just trying to stay calm.
        1.5 to 4
        Normal and engaged.
        Over 4
        Very animated. Reads as excited or upset, depending on loudness and words.

        Loudness

        Measured in decibels (dB). Talya looks at whether you got louder or quieter from the start of the clip to the end, and how loud you were compared to your calm voice.

        +3 dB
        Just noticeable.
        +6 dB
        Clearly louder. Building volume is one of the most common signs a talk is turning into a fight.
        −3 dB or less
        Trailing off. Can sound unsure, hurt or like you are giving up on the point.

        How close you hold the microphone changes loudness, so keep the same distance as when you recorded your baseline.

        Sentence endings

        Whether your voice goes up or down on the last words.

        Rising
        Sounds like a question. Can come across as unsure, or as "right?" pressure.
        Falling
        Sounds finished and certain. A sharp drop can sound curt.
        Level
        Neutral.

        Pauses

        The share of time you stop talking for a third of a second or longer.

        Under 8%
        Hardly any breaks. Can feel like a lecture, with no room to respond.
        8 to 35%
        Natural.
        Over 35%
        Lots of stopping. Thoughtful, or hesitant and uncomfortable.

        The picture

        Time runs left to right. Gray bars show how loud you were at each moment. Green dots show how high your voice was: higher dots mean a higher voice.

        Look for patterns: dots climbing toward the end means your voice rose as you went. Bars getting taller means you got louder. A flat row of dots means a monotone.

        Tone tags

        Warm, even
        Normal pace and steady volume, with natural pitch movement.
        Tense / pressured
        Fast, and higher or louder than your calm voice.
        Escalating
        Getting louder or higher as you go.
        Flat / withdrawn
        Very little pitch movement and even volume.
        Soft / apologetic
        Slower and quieter than your calm voice.
        Questioning
        Rising endings.
        Neutral
        Nothing stands out.

        What Talya can't do

        • It hears how you speak, not what you say or mean.
        • It can't tell sarcasm, jokes or a cold from real feelings.
        • Accents, background noise and a phone held too far away all change results.
        • It never decides who is right, and it is not therapy or a diagnosis.
        • That is why confidence never goes above Moderate.

        Recording not working?

        Nothing happens, or it says access is blocked
        • Use the real site, not a preview. Recording only works at bawcejawn.github.io/Talya (or in the app), not inside a preview window.
        • Allow the microphone. In Chrome or Edge, click the icon left of the web address, set Microphone to Allow, and reload. In Safari on a Mac, go to Safari → Settings → Websites → Microphone.
        • On a Mac, also open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and turn on your browser.
        • On Windows, open Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone and allow desktop apps and your browser.
        • Close Zoom, Teams or FaceTime if they are using the microphone.
        It records, but says it couldn't hear me
        • Watch the green bar while you speak. If it barely moves, your computer may be using the wrong microphone. Check your sound settings for the input device.
        • Sit about an arm's length away and talk at normal volume for at least 5 seconds.
        • Or use your phone: it usually has a better microphone for this.
